# Governance principles

[en](GOVERNANCE.md) · [uk](../uk/GOVERNANCE.md) · [home](../../README.md)

This is an initial governance framework. A ratified governance model will be
developed openly as the contributor community forms.

## Commitments

1. **Public-interest mission.** Decisions should preserve the project's ability
   to serve humanity rather than a single state, company or ideology.
2. **Scientific integrity.** Evidence, methods, assumptions, uncertainty and
   corrections remain visible.
3. **Open deliberation.** Material decisions are proposed and recorded publicly,
   except where privacy or security requires a narrow confidential process.
4. **Independence.** Funding or data access cannot purchase a preferred finding,
   exclusive truth or silent veto.
5. **Plural evidence.** Competing credible estimates and disputed control claims
   may coexist with their provenance.
6. **Reversibility.** Early decisions should remain easy to revise as evidence
   and the community improve.
7. **No harmful disclosure.** Openness does not require publishing personal data,
   security-sensitive details or unlawfully obtained information.

## Provisional decision process

- Significant changes begin as a public proposal.
- The proposal states scope, evidence, alternatives, risks and conflicts.
- Relevant experts and affected contributors are invited to review it.
- Maintainers document the decision and unresolved objections.
- Scientific claims may not be decided by popularity alone.
- Contributors can request reconsideration with new evidence.

## Roles to establish

- maintainers responsible for repository quality and releases;
- scientific stewards responsible for methods and review;
- data stewards responsible for provenance, licensing and quality;
- community stewards responsible for participation and conduct;
- an independent advisory group spanning disciplines and regions.

No permanent roles have been appointed yet.

## Funding transparency

Material grants, sponsorships and in-kind contributions should be disclosed with
their purpose, restrictions and relevant conflicts. Publicly funded outputs
should remain public whenever legally possible.

## Licensing decision

Code, documentation and datasets may require different licenses. The project
will select them through a public proposal considering reuse, attribution,
copyleft, patents, database rights, privacy and compatibility with source data.
